# Feasibility analysis — 2026-09-25

## Where things actually stand

This is a light-touch review: only one day has passed since the work plan was re-baselined to v0.3
(`b9dead2`, 2026-09-24), and the four commits since the last review (`92beb2c`, also 2026-09-24) are
almost entirely process, not build. `32a5cd5` is the one commit with real engineering in it — it pushes
the Input Parser from v4 to v6, resolves a genuinely hard definitional question (what counts as
`intent`) through two rounds of blind human annotation, and then spends the freshly-settled prompt on the
held-out split. The result: **every per-run threshold passed except one — `intent` agreement across 3
repeated runs came in at 93.9 %, just under the 95 % bar — so E5.1 stays open.** The other three commits
are the v0.3 re-baseline itself (`dd816f2`, `b9dead2`) and a progress-site parser update (`24243ea`, not
a tracked task). Test suite verified now in a plain Python 3.11 venv (no `resto` conda env in this
container; `sumo` from the `eclipse-sumo` PyPI wheel put on `PATH` instead): 892 passed, 1 skipped
(a fixture needing a locally-generated run directory), `ruff` clean, `mypy` clean.

So today's honest read is: the tracker's shape changed a lot (v0.3's story points, the ⏳ status, four new
tasks, all-new milestone dates), but the actual frontier of the project — what got *built* — barely
moved. That's expected the day after a re-baseline; it's not a signal of slowing down.

## Pace vs. the v0.3 calendar

v0.3 gives itself real room: 38.5 pts/week against ≈574 remaining points to 18 Feb, +170 pts of slack.
By my own recount (cross-checked against the plan's own stated total), ✅ tasks carry 362 of 954 plan
points (37.9 %), ⏳ carries 36, 🔄 carries 14 — 43 % of the plan's points are at least started or done. For
a project one week past its foundations milestone in wall-clock terms (M0 closed 2026-09-14), that is a
fast pace, and it's the reason v0.3 exists at all: the team ran far enough ahead of v0.2's calendar that
the whole plan needed re-baselining upward. I read that history as the single strongest piece of evidence
for feasibility — this isn't a team estimating optimistically from zero, it's a team that has already
beaten its own estimates once.

The near-term milestone, **M2** (target Fri 9 Oct, deadline Fri 13 Nov), sits on a genuinely short
critical chain — E3.8 → E4.4, 34 points, under a single week of capacity. Its target is two weeks out and
nothing on that chain has started (checked directly: `eval/dev-net/demand/*.trips.xml` still departs in
`[0, 3600)` seconds, not clock time; `DEFAULT_WINDOW` in the question generator is still relative). One
day after the re-baseline that's not a red flag by itself, but E3.8 needs to actually start this week to
keep the target real rather than aspirational — it is wave 1a's first item and gates everything else
downstream (E4.2–E4.4's dev sweeps, E3.7, and by extension the whole Coordinator spine).

## M2, M3, M5 — the three the plan says must not be cut

- **M2** (Expert built on DEV-NET): mathematically comfortable (34 pts vs. a 2-week runway at 38.5
  pts/week), but entirely unstarted. Low risk *if* E3.8 opens this week; the risk is inattention, not
  scope.
- **M3** (end-to-end loop built): the plan's own §4.4 calls the Coordinator spine (E3.8 → E3.7 → E5.2 →
  E5.3 → E7.1 → E7.2/E6.7/E7.3) "the critical path of the whole plan," and it's right — this single chain
  also feeds M4 and M6. Two things concern me here that aren't visible from the point totals alone: (1)
  **ADR-0027 is still Proposed**, and E5.13 (accepting or revising it) sits right after E3.8 on the
  critical path, before E3.7 can even start; (2) the one data point I have this review for how long a
  "settle a definition with the user" cycle takes — the `intent` convention — took roughly two days and
  eight dated log entries in `evaluating-resto.md` §5, with two rounds of blind annotation, before the
  Parser could even be re-tuned against it. If ADR-0027's acceptance turns out to need similar back-and-
  forth (arms/contrasts is a comparably subtle domain concept), that's exactly the kind of overhead that
  doesn't show up as "points" anywhere in the plan and could eat into the slack faster than the point
  budget suggests. Worth watching, not yet worth acting on.
- **M5** (thesis result built): depends on a REAL-NET chain (E6.3 → E6.6 → E3.5 → E3.6 → E4.8, 60 pts)
  that has no prerequisite and could start in parallel with the Coordinator spine — the plan is explicit
  that E6.3 "can start any week." None of it has started yet, but there's also no reason it can't run
  concurrently with wave 1a/1b once someone picks it up, which is what keeps the ≈10-week runway to its
  Dec 4 target comfortable on paper.

None of the three looks structurally endangered today. The one real signal from this review — E5.1
missing its held-out bar on the first attempt — is informative about *how* these milestones might slip,
not *whether*: it shows that even a heavily-tuned, well-evaluated agent (six prompt versions, a 306→346-
request bank, two rounds of human annotation) can still miss a DoD threshold on the first real
measurement. E4.2–E4.4's thresholds (diagnostic Jaccard ≥0.6, counterfactual direction ≥75 %/band ≥50 %)
are not obviously easier than Parser intent agreement, and E5.5's Coordinator routing bar (≥90 %) is in
the same family. That's not a reason to doubt the plan — the fallback order (§5) exists precisely for
this, and step 2 (shrink measurement scale, never below 2 repetitions) or step 4.4 (ablation → calibration
only) are cheap, pre-planned ways to absorb exactly this kind of miss without touching M2/M3/M5. It *is* a
reason to expect more than zero threshold misses between now and Validation 2, and to treat that as
normal rather than alarming when it happens.

## Bottleneck observed this review

The concrete bottleneck isn't capacity, it's **decision latency on domain definitions that the plan
prices as instantaneous.** E3.8 (10 pts) and E5.13 (2 pts) look cheap in the plan, but E5.13 specifically
is "accept or change-then-accept ADR-0027" — a decision, not a build task — and the one comparable
decision this project has made (the `intent` semantics) took multiple days of iteration despite being, on
its face, simpler than arms-and-contrasts. If E5.13 runs long, it stalls E3.7, E5.2, E5.4, and E6.7
simultaneously, which is worse than its 2-point price tag implies because it sits at the fork of three
different downstream chains.

## Recommendation

**Keep going as planned.** No step of the work plan §5 fallback order is warranted yet — the plan was
just re-baselined with real slack, the near-term math is comfortable, and the one miss found this review
(E5.1's held-out intent agreement) is a normal tuning outcome with a cheap absorption path already in the
plan, not a milestone-level problem. The concrete action item is to start E3.8 immediately (it is wave
1a's first item and nothing downstream can proceed without it) and to watch — without yet acting on —
how quickly E5.13 (ADR-0027 acceptance) resolves once it's picked up, since that is the one place this
review found a real, plan-invisible risk to the Coordinator-spine critical path that feeds M3/M4/M6
together.
