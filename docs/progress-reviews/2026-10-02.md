# Feasibility analysis, 2026-10-02

## Where things actually stand

Twenty-one commits landed on 01.10.2026, the day after the last review, and they close one real gap
while opening a bigger architectural question than they answer. I read the two new ADRs in full rather
than trusting their one-line summaries in the work plan, because a same-day Accepted status on a
document this consequential is exactly the kind of thing a shallow pass would wave through.

First, the straightforward good news: E3.11 is actually done. The last review left it at "ticket 1 of
several," and today's five remaining tickets landed in sequence: DEV-NET's demands described,
`Question.network_only` added and tested, the request bank rewritten for the ADR (including three
concepts for networks that are not in the database at all, which is a real edge case, not a token
gesture), the variants regenerated, and the Parser's contract change shipped as v7. I did not take the
work plan's own "DoD met" framing on faith. I opened `v7-dev.json` directly and read the numbers myself:
255 requests, every one of E5.1's actual dev thresholds cleared, the new fields (`network_only`,
`demand_ref`, `time_window`) reported honestly with their misses still visible rather than smoothed
over. That is a clean, verifiable close, and I flipped it to ✅ with evidence, not on narrative.

Second, the thing I am less comfortable just filing as progress: ADR-0037, Accepted the same day, rips
the four database tools out of the Coordinator and pushes that responsibility onto the Network Author
and Demand Generator specialists instead. The stated reason is sound: a plan bank graded against
database lookups would be testing the wrong thing, and the Coordinator duplicating knowledge the
specialists need anyway is a real design smell. But look at the pattern across the last week. ADR-0032
(Expert network scope), ADR-0035 (described demand) and now ADR-0037 all describe themselves as found
while trying to actually start E3.7. Three architecture-level discoveries in eight days, every one of
them triggered by opening the plan-bank task rather than by building it. That is not evidence of a
project drifting. The ADRs are well-reasoned and the fixes are real. But it is evidence that E3.7 keeps
functioning as a trigger for redesigning its neighbours instead of landing. ADR-0037 alone adds a
brand-new task (E5.14, 5 points) between E3.11 and E3.7 on the critical chain, so even though E3.11
just closed, the chain did not get shorter: it has the same eight hops it had yesterday, with a step
swapped in for a step that didn't exist. None of E5.14, the reshaped E3.7, the reshaped E5.2, or either
specialist's new resolution duty has a single commit behind it yet.

Third, ADR-0036 (raw demand data) grew E6 by four tasks and 26 points (106 → 137, +29 %) on the same
day, for a module where nothing has been built yet: every E6 agent file is still the original 4-line
placeholder, confirmed again by grep this review. I am not alleging scope creep in the pejorative
sense; the work plan's own accounting absorbs all of it (+37 points total, deadline unchanged) and the
maintainer is clearly tracking the arithmetic carefully. But "absorbed on paper" and "absorbed in
practice" are different claims, and E6 is now the largest section in the plan by a wide margin with
zero lines of code to show for any of it.

The test suite is in good shape and I verified it myself rather than trusting the tracker's prose from
the last review: 1298 passed (up from 1261), 1 skipped for the same pre-existing fixture gap, `ruff`
clean, `mypy` clean over 313 source files. `pip install -e ".[dev]"` worked cleanly in a fresh venv, no
conda environment available in this container (same limitation every review has noted; I ran pytest and
ruff and mypy directly against system Python 3.11 instead, which is the fallback this skill specifies).

## Pace vs. the v0.3 calendar

The work plan re-baselined M2's own target today, from Fri 9 Oct to Tue 20 Oct, with the deadline
untouched. M2 was already met on 2026-09-25, so this doesn't change anything that was actually at risk.
It just makes the "ahead of schedule" margin look bigger (25 days instead of 11) for a milestone that
was never in question. I want to name that plainly rather than let it pass as more good news than it
is: moving a target later, after it has already been cleared, is bookkeeping, not acceleration. It is
not dishonest. Nothing is being hidden, and the deadline, the number that actually matters, did not
move. But a string of "ahead of target" lines is easy to read as more momentum than it is when one of
the targets moved to produce that reading.

The slack trend is the number I'd actually watch. It has gone +170 → +158 → +121 over the last several
reviews as E3.11, E6.8–E6.11 and ADR-0037's net changes have been discovered and folded in. Each
individual addition has been small and well-justified; the direction is still fine (+121 points against
roughly 745 points of capacity to 18 Feb is a real cushion, not a thin one), but three consecutive drops
is a pattern, not noise, and it is worth tracking whether it keeps falling at this rate or whether
today's changes are the last big discovery in this cluster.

E6.3 (REAL-NET freeze) is the one item I flagged last review as a free, prerequisite-free task sitting
untouched, and it is still untouched today: still zero commits, still no `real-net.*` file. That was
true before E6 grew by 31 points; it is more conspicuous now that the section it belongs to has nearly
quadrupled relative to E6.1/E6.2's original point count. Nothing about today's commits makes E6.3 more
urgent on the calendar (it still isn't due until 6 Jan at the latest), but the gap between "has no
excuse to be idle" and "is in fact idle" has not closed in over a week.

## M2, M3, M5: the three the plan says must not be cut

- **M2** (Expert built on DEV-NET). Unaffected by anything in today's commits beyond the cosmetic target
  move described above. Still solidly met.
- **M3** (end-to-end loop built). This is where today's changes actually land, and the honest read is
  "net roughly flat, not improved." E3.11 finishing is real progress on the chain, but ADR-0037 inserted
  E5.14 right behind it, and reshaped E3.7 and E5.2 themselves, so the chain is still eight tasks long
  with zero of the post-E3.11 tasks built. The 6 Nov target is five weeks out and wave 1b (which carries
  all of E5.14, E3.7, E5.2, E5.3, E5.4, E7.1) doesn't open on the plan's own calendar until 12 Oct, so
  nothing here is yet a lateness signal. But it is now a slightly longer and more architecturally
  unsettled chain than it was two days ago, not a shorter one.
- **M5** (thesis result built). Unchanged in substance and still the milestone I'd call out first if
  asked where the real risk sits. Its 60-point chain (E6.3 → E6.6 → E3.5 → E3.6 → E4.8) runs entirely
  through E6, which today grew by 29 % in planned scope while contributing zero actual commits. E6.3
  itself remains the one piece of schedule risk in this whole review that has no calendar excuse and no
  technical blocker. It could have started any day in the last two weeks and has not.

## Recommendation

Keep going as planned. Nothing here crosses into needing any step of the §5 fallback order, and the
arithmetic (slack still +121 of ~745 points of capacity, deadline unmoved) backs that. But I'd put two
sharper asks on the table than the last review did, because "the spine is moving, just not as finished
code" stops being a reasonable thing to say after the third same-day ADR in eight days:

1. **The plan-bank task needs to actually start.** E3.7 has now triggered three ADRs without landing a
   single `eval/plan_bank/`-shaped file. ADR-0037 is a sound decision and I'm not second-guessing it,
   but if a fourth review in a row opens with "E3.7 still hasn't started, but here is what we learned
   trying," that is the point to pick it up directly rather than keep discovering around it.
2. **E6.3 should stop being the item every review repeats.** It has no prerequisite, no identified
   technical risk, and a real downstream chain (feeding M4 and M5 both) waiting on it. It has been
   idle long enough, across enough reviews, that "not due yet" is starting to do more work as an excuse
   than the calendar actually supports this many times in a row.

Neither of these is a crisis. There is still slack, still five-plus weeks before M3's target, still
until May in the worst case per the standing fallback. They are the two places I would spend attention
next, specifically because they are the two places where "the plan accounts for it" and "the work
exists" have started to diverge.
